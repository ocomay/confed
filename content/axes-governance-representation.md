# Spectrum Axes: Governance & Political Representation

**Summary**: Detailed comparison of 4 axes from [[confederation-spectrum-framework]] — parliamentary structure, Palestinian political unity, Gaza governance/legitimacy, and self-determination symmetry — across the five reference configurations (status quo, full annexation, state of all its citizens, A Land for All, two states for two peoples).

**Sources**: See [[confederation-spectrum-framework]] for the full sourcing note and reliability caveats applying to all five configuration columns.

**Last updated**: 2026-09-03

---

## Parliamentary Structure — powers of each elected body, and who elects whom

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Multiple non-overlapping, partly defunct bodies: the **Knesset** (120 seats, national PR) is the sole sovereign legislature, elected by all Israeli citizens including Palestinian citizens of Israel. The **Palestinian Legislative Council** has not been elected since **2006**; the PA presidency not since 2005 — PA authority in any case limited to civil matters in Areas A/B only. **Area C** (60% of the West Bank) is administered directly by the Israeli military/COGAT, with its Palestinian residents holding **no vote in the body that actually governs them** (neither Knesset nor an effective PLC). **Gaza** has been governed since 2026 by an international transitional technocratic committee (NCAG) with **no elected body at all** (see [[final-status-core-disputes]]) |
| **2. Full Annexation** | **Knesset remains the sole sovereign legislature.** The central, usually-unaddressed question: do Palestinian residents of annexed territory get the vote? Explicit right-wing annexation proposals generally answer **no** — specifically to preserve a Jewish majority in the Knesset — making this the starkest, most formalized "governed but unrepresented" outcome of the five columns, converting today's *de facto* Area C disenfranchisement into a permanent, explicit constitutional feature |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | **Single unified parliament** (successor to the Knesset), elected by universal citizen suffrage on a one-person-one-vote basis, with **no ethnic/national curia, communal veto, or second representative layer** — all sovereign legislative power resides in this one body, consistent with the model's rejection of communal/national political categories altogether (see the self-determination-symmetry row below) |
| **4. A Land for All** | **Two fully separate sovereign parliaments** — Knesset and a Palestinian parliament — each with full domestic legislative sovereignty over its own citizens. Voting is **citizenship-based, not residence-based**: Palestinians residing in Israel vote in Palestinian elections; Israelis residing in Palestine vote in Israeli elections (per Wikipedia detail, [[a-land-for-all]]). **Plus a confederative joint layer** — a joint Israeli-Palestinian assembly for shared-competence disputes — whose exact powers (binding vs. advisory, seat allocation, veto rules) are **not specified** in sources reviewed, **needs verification**. A distinct wrinkle: **Arab citizens of Israel would hold dual citizenship of both states**, raising an unresolved question of whether they'd vote in *both* parliaments — an unusual mechanic with no equivalent in any other column |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | **Two fully separate, non-overlapping sovereign parliaments** — Knesset for Israel, a Palestinian parliament for Palestine — each citizen votes only in their own state's elections. **No joint assembly, no shared sovereign body, and no dual-citizenship/dual-voting mechanism** — a cleaner, more total separation than ALFA specifically because it lacks both the confederative joint layer and the dual-citizenship complication |

## Palestinian Political Unity

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Fatah/PA controls the West Bank only; no general elections since 2006; Gaza now under an international technocratic committee excluding both Hamas and the PA (see [[final-status-core-disputes]]) |
| **2. Full Annexation** | Largely irrelevant to this model by design — unilateral annexation does not require or envision a sovereign Palestinian counterpart at all |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | Palestinian political factions would need to reconstitute as ordinary parties within one state's domestic politics rather than a separate national government — a fundamental reframing, not merely an unresolved question |
| **4. A Land for All** | **Assumes, but does not specify**, a unified Palestinian negotiating partner — a gap already flagged in [[final-status-core-disputes]] |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Classic two-state proposals generally **presuppose** a resolution to the Fatah-Hamas split (via reconciliation or Hamas's political marginalization/disarmament) as a precondition for a negotiating partner — explicit in most such frameworks even if not always achieved |

## Gaza Governance / Legitimacy

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Under international transitional administration (Board of Peace / National Committee for the Administration of Gaza) as of 2026 — governed by neither Hamas nor the PA directly; see [[final-status-core-disputes]] |
| **2. Full Annexation** | Typically **not addressed** — right-wing annexation proposals generally target West Bank Area C specifically; Gaza is usually treated as a separate question outside the annexation framework |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | Presumably absorbed as state territory, but the practical integration of Gaza's population (2+ million, no current civil integration with Israel) is not theorized in sources reviewed — a major undertheorized gap flagged for this column specifically |
| **4. A Land for All** | **Not addressed at all** in the platform (a gap already flagged in [[final-status-core-disputes]]) |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Gaza integrated as part of the Palestinian state alongside the West Bank, typically via a proposed territorial corridor/land link — a standard, specific feature of classic two-state proposals not found in ALFA's documented platform |

## Self-Determination Symmetry

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Israel's Basic Law claims self-determination "unique to the Jewish people" (Sec. 1.C, see [[nation-state-law-tension]]); Palestinians hold no sovereign self-determination |
| **2. Full Annexation** | Further entrenches asymmetry by explicit design — the model does not extend Palestinian self-determination at all |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | Rejects ethno-national self-determination claims **for either people** — a third position distinct from "symmetric self-determination," instead proposing no privileged national self-determination for anyone, only individual civic rights |
| **4. A Land for All** | Formally symmetric: "the two people will realize their right of self-determination" (2014 platform language, [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]]) |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Formally symmetric: each people obtains its own fully sovereign nation-state — symmetric in form, though critics note Palestinian sovereignty is often *security-limited* in practice (see [[axes-territory-borders-security]]) even where formally recognized |

## Key Cross-Axis Observation

**"No representation" is already the status quo for part of the population, not a hypothetical risk of any proposed model.** Area C Palestinians and Gaza residents currently have no functioning vote in whichever body actually governs them. Full annexation's central, often-unstated design choice is to make this **permanent and explicit** rather than transitional. Conversely, A Land for All introduces a genuinely novel mechanic — dual citizenship with a possible dual vote for Arab citizens of Israel — that has no equivalent in either the status quo or the classic two-state model, and whose implications are not fully specified in sources reviewed.

**Gaza governance and Palestinian political unity are weak/undertheorized across every column except the status quo** — none of the four alternative models has a well-sourced, specific answer for how Gaza's current internationally-administered status or the Fatah-Hamas split gets resolved.
