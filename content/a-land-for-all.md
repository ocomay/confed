# A Land for All (ארץ לכולם — "Two States, One Homeland")

**Summary**: A joint Israeli-Palestinian political initiative, founded 2012, proposing a two-state confederation as the resolution to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. Co-led by May Pundak and Dr. Rula Hardal.

## Basic Facts

**Name**: ארץ לכולם (Arabic: بلاد للجميع); also known as "Two States, One Homeland"

**Founded**: **2012**, under joint Israeli-Palestinian leadership. Emerged from a series of meetings between **Meron Rappaport**, an Israeli journalist, and **Awni Al-Mahshni**, a Palestinian activist. Other Palestinian and Israeli journalists, scholars, and activists gradually joined to formulate a statement of shared principles for coexistence.

**Leadership**: Co-CEOs are **May Pundak** (Israeli Jew, daughter of Ron Pundak — a figure associated with the Oslo Accords back-channel negotiations) and **Dr. Rula Hardal** (Palestinian citizen of Israel).

**Composition**: Israeli Jews, Palestinian citizens of Israel, and Palestinians.

**Membership organization**: [Alliance for Middle East Peace](https://www.allmep.org/allmep_member/a-land-for-all/).

## ⚠️ Correction to Source Interview

The Jan 2025 interview (`eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview.md`) presents **Oren Yiftachel and Rula Hardal** as the two figures representing the organization, and Yiftachel states the organization is "12 years ago" old (implying ~2013). Cross-referencing Wikipedia:

1. **Founding date**: 2012, not ~2013 — minor discrepancy, likely rounding in speech.
2. **Leadership structure**: The actual co-CEOs are **May Pundak and Rula Hardal** — not Yiftachel. Yiftachel is a co-founder and prominent academic spokesperson, but **May Pundak is entirely unmentioned in the interview** despite being one of the organization's two chief executives. This is a notable gap in the interview's self-presentation.
3. **Founding figures**: The interview doesn't mention Meron Rappaport or Awni Al-Mahshni at all, instead implying the founding was more academically-driven (Yiftachel's framing). Wikipedia's account centers a journalist-activist origin story instead.

## Recognition & Political Reception

- Won the **Luxembourg Peace Prize** ("Outstanding Peace Support Award"), 2021
- Proposal presented at the **2025 UN High-Level Conference** on "The Peaceful Settlement of the Question of Palestine and the Implementation of the Two-State Solution"
- **November 2025**: Proposal adopted by **Standing Together** (Israeli-Palestinian social justice movement)
- **June 2026**: Standing Together launched political party ***Makom Lekulanu*** (מקום לכולנו, "a place for us all") — explicitly described (per cited Forward article) as evoking A Land for All's Hebrew name, *Eretz Lekulam*

**None of this reception/recognition is mentioned in the Jan 2025 interview** — either because it postdates the interview (UN conference, Standing Together adoption, new party) or because the interview's framing emphasizes quiet, embattled advocacy work rather than institutional recognition already achieved by that point (Luxembourg Prize, 2021).

## The Platform in Full Detail (per Wikipedia — significantly more specific than the interview)

This is the organization's actual stated platform, which is considerably more detailed and in some places **different in emphasis** from how Yiftachel and Hardal summarized it in the interview. See inline comparisons.

### Territorial structure
Two states within the **pre-1967 Green Line borders**, forming a confederation — i.e., NOT a novel border configuration, but the standard "1967 lines" territorial baseline used across most two-state proposals. *(The interview did not specify this explicitly.)*

### Voting and citizenship
- Palestinians residing in Israel vote in **Palestinian national elections**; Israeli citizens residing in the Palestinian state vote in **Israeli elections** — i.e., political rights follow national affiliation, not physical residence.
- **Arab Israelis would be dual citizens of both states** — a specific mechanism not mentioned in the interview at all.

### Refugees and right of return — more nuanced than the interview suggested
- The future Palestinian state would have the **sovereign right to grant Palestinian refugees the right of return** (to Palestine).
- **Diaspora Jews would maintain the right to return to Israel** (Aliyah) — stated as a parallel/counterpart provision.
- Critically: **Palestinian refugees could return to Palestine, and would gradually have an *option* to reside in Israel** — this is a gradual pathway to Israeli residency for refugees, which is **more expansive than what the interview conveyed**. The interview (Yiftachel, 10:46–11:14) described return as vested only in the Palestinian state with "freedom of movement" for visiting/working — it did not mention a gradual residency option in Israel itself. **This is a meaningful discrepancy** worth flagging: the actual platform appears to go further toward enabling some refugees to live in Israel (not just visit) than the interview's summary implied.
- Israeli settlers living in Palestine would **remain citizens of Israel** (not become Palestinian citizens, contrary to how the interview's "under Palestinian law" framing might be read).

### Settlers — clarified mechanism
Illegal Israeli settlers in the occupied territories would become **Israeli residents of Palestine** (not Palestinian citizens), provided they accept and abide by Palestinian sovereign law. They would have the right to vote for **local authorities** (not national Palestinian elections). The plan explicitly calls for a **freeze on Israeli settlement expansion**, and gradual implementation of expanded Israeli residency in Palestine "to avoid overwhelming the Palestinian state with Israeli citizens" — i.e., the platform itself acknowledges the demographic-overwhelm risk that critics raise (see [[confederation-model-critique]]).

There will be **no Israeli military presence** in the Palestinian state.

### Jerusalem — RESOLVED via primary source (see [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]])
Wikipedia states Jerusalem would be managed by "international authorities and religious representatives." **This has now been checked against the organization's own 2014 founding platform text directly, which says instead**: "Jerusalem will serve as the capital of both states... A special municipal government will be established in it, **managed jointly and equally by both states**" — i.e., **joint Israeli-Palestinian** municipal government, not international/religious third-party administration. This is much closer to Yiftachel's "Brussels" comparison in the interview (a city shared between national communities) than to Wikipedia's characterization. **Wikipedia's "international authorities and religious representatives" framing appears to be inaccurate or sourced from a different document** — treat the primary-source "joint, equal Israeli-Palestinian management" version as authoritative going forward. See [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] for full detail and side-by-side comparison.

### Security
- Each state has **full security responsibility within its own sovereign territory**, plus a security partnership between the two states.
- Independent security forces operate solely within their own territories, but with close cooperation on security, intelligence, and policing.
- Freedom of movement is qualified: both countries **reserve the right to deny entry to hostile individuals and conduct border checks** — i.e., borders are open but not unconditionally so.
- Both states commit to **demilitarization agreements**.
- Dispute resolution: a **joint Israeli-Palestinian assembly** or a **joint human rights court**.

### Equal rights and Palestinian citizens of Israel
Citizens of both states have equal rights per the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights**. Explicit commitments to:
- Amending discriminatory laws affecting Palestinian citizens of Israel
- Equal representation and equitable resource allocation
- Legal resolutions for construction, **including recognition of Bedouin communities in the Negev**

**This entire equal-rights/Palestinian-citizens-of-Israel dimension of the platform was not mentioned at all in the interview** — a significant omission, since it's one of the platform's more concrete and domestically-relevant commitments (addressing conditions for Palestinian citizens of Israel today, not just the future confederation structure).

## Assessment: Interview vs. Actual Platform

Comparing the Jan 2025 interview against the fuller platform documented on Wikipedia, the interview:
- **Simplified or omitted** several concrete mechanisms (Green Line baseline, dual citizenship for Arab Israelis, gradual refugee residency option in Israel, Jerusalem's joint Israeli-Palestinian municipal government — see correction above, equal-rights commitments for Palestinian citizens of Israel)
- **Did not mention** the organization's actual CEO (May Pundak) or its institutional recognition (Luxembourg Prize, UN presentation)
- Presented a **broadly accurate but less precise** version of the core confederation mechanics (two states + shared institutions + freedom of movement), consistent with the fuller platform in its general shape

This pattern is consistent with an **interview format naturally compressing and personalizing** a detailed institutional platform — not necessarily evidence of misrepresentation, but a reminder that **the interview should not be treated as the authoritative statement of A Land for All's platform**. See [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]] for the reconciled/fuller picture.
