# Spectrum Axes: Citizenship, Refugees & Rights

**Summary**: Detailed comparison of 3 axes from [[confederation-spectrum-framework]] — citizenship/immigration symmetry, refugee return scope, and Palestinian citizens of Israel's equality — across the five reference configurations.

**Sources**: See [[confederation-spectrum-framework]] for the full sourcing note and reliability caveats applying to all five configuration columns.

**Last updated**: 2026-09-03

---

## Citizenship / Immigration Symmetry

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Sharply asymmetric: Israeli Law of Return for Jews worldwide; Palestinians in the territories hold no path to Israeli citizenship (Oslo did not create one); West Bank Palestinians hold PA-issued documents, not full sovereign citizenship |
| **2. Full Annexation** | Contested and politically live: most annexation proposals from the right do **not** extend full voting citizenship to Palestinian residents of annexed territory, in order to preserve a Jewish demographic majority — this is widely noted as the central internal tension of annexation-without-rights |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | Fully symmetric and unified: single citizenship for all residents under one civic code, no religious/ethnic distinction in law |
| **4. A Land for All** | Explicitly separate by design: each state has "the right to determine... their citizenship laws... as they see fit" (2014 platform, quoted in [[nation-state-law-tension]]) — Law of Return preserved for Israel, parallel Palestinian state discretion over refugee citizenship |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Separate citizenship codes for two fully sovereign states; Israel's Law of Return continues; Palestinian state has its own citizenship law — similar end-state to ALFA on this specific axis, but arrived at via *separation* rather than *confederative parallel-preservation* |

## Refugee Return Scope

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | No return; refugees remain in camps/diaspora/West Bank/Gaza under UNRWA registration |
| **2. Full Annexation** | No return; would likely further foreclose any return mechanism given the annexationist premise |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | In principle the most expansive of the five — no demographic/national-character rationale exists in this model to limit return, though some Bishara-associated formulations remain vague on the mechanics — **needs verification** |
| **4. A Land for All** | Right of return formally to the Palestinian state; **gradual option to reside** (not gain citizenship) in Israel itself (see [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]]) |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Per the Clinton Parameters model: the Palestinian state is the "focal point" for returning refugees; Israel may accept a limited, Israeli-determined number at its own discretion; **no specific right of return to Israel** is recognized |

## Palestinian Citizens of Israel — Equality

| Configuration | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Status Quo** | Documented disparities in resource allocation and planning; unrecognized Bedouin villages in the Negev; Nation-State Law downgraded Arabic from official-language status |
| **2. Full Annexation** | Likely most acute under this model — expands the population living under Jewish-majority sovereignty without full citizenship rights, if West Bank Palestinians are also not enfranchised |
| **3. State of All Its Citizens** | Full equality is the organizing principle of this model by definition — the core reason for its existence |
| **4. A Land for All** | Explicit platform commitments: "national minority rights, civil equality, appropriate representation," including recognition of Bedouin communities in the Negev (verified quote, [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]]) |
| **5. Two States for Two Peoples** | Generally **not addressed directly** — this is treated as an internal Israeli civil-rights matter separate from the core two-state territorial deal, though individual two-state advocates often support it as a parallel domestic reform |

## Key Cross-Axis Observation

On citizenship specifically, **A Land for All and classic Two States arrive at similar formal end-states** (separate citizenship codes, Israel's Law of Return preserved) **via different logics** — ALFA frames this as a deliberate, symmetric, confederative choice ("as they see fit" for both states equally), while the classic model simply treats it as a natural consequence of full partition. The practical difference shows up more in the *refugee return* and *settler status* axes (see [[axes-territory-borders-security]]) than in citizenship law itself.

**"State of All Its Citizens" is the only configuration where equality for Palestinian citizens of Israel is structurally guaranteed rather than negotiated** — in every other column, this is either a documented ongoing problem (status quo), a likely-worsening one (annexation), a stated aspiration requiring platform commitments to enforce (A Land for All), or simply outside scope (classic two-state).
