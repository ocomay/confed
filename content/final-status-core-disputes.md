# The Ten Core Disputes of the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict

**Summary**: Framework page listing the ten central, hardest disputes underlying the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, serving as background context for evaluating any proposed solution (including [[israel-palestine-confederation-model|the confederation model]]). Cross-referenced against existing wiki pages where already covered.

**Sources**: General knowledge synthesis + targeted web research for Gaza governance and Fatah-Hamas division (see citations inline). Other items draw on established, widely-documented facts about the conflict's core final-status issues; **specific claims flagged for verification where sourcing is thin**.

**Last updated**: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Borders and Territory

**The question**: Where does the border run? The 1967 "Green Line" is the internationally-referenced baseline, but Israel has built substantial settlement blocs (Ma'ale Adumim, Gush Etzion, Ariel) that any realistic map would need to address via land swaps or annexation.

**Relevance to confederation**: [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] shows A Land for All's platform assumes the Green Line as the baseline — but doesn't specify land-swap mechanics, leaving this core question formally unresolved even within the confederation proposal.

## 2. Jerusalem and the Holy Basin

**The question**: Not just east/west division — sovereignty over the **Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif** itself (access, prayer rights, administration) is arguably the single most religiously and politically explosive sub-issue in the entire conflict.

**Relevance to confederation**: See [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] for the specific proposal (joint Israeli-Palestinian municipal government per the 2014 primary text) and the discrepancy with Wikipedia's differing characterization — already a live example of how even sympathetic sources disagree on the mechanics.

## 3. Palestinian Refugees and the Right of Return

**The question**: An estimated 5-6 million registered Palestinian refugees and descendants (per UNRWA registration — **needs direct verification of current figure**), claiming an individual right of return to specific pre-1948 homes now inside Israel. This runs directly against Israeli concern for preserving a Jewish demographic majority.

**Relevance to confederation**: Extensively covered — see [[confederation-model-critique]] (Chacar's critique that confederation curtails rather than honors return) and [[nation-state-law-tension]] (the "as they see fit" citizenship-discretion mechanism as the proposed resolution).

## 4. Israeli Settlements in the West Bank

**The question**: Over 500,000 Israeli settlers now live in the West Bank (**figure needs direct verification against a current demographic source** — commonly cited range). Questions of legality (the ICJ's July 2024 advisory opinion, referenced in [[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview]]), evacuation vs. permanent residency under Palestinian sovereignty, and territorial contiguity for a Palestinian state all hinge on this.

**Relevance to confederation**: Central to the model — see [[confederation-model-critique]] (settler governance risk) and [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] (settlers as "permanent residents of Palestine," not citizens).

## 5. Mutual Recognition — "The Jewish State" Demand

**The question**: Since roughly the Netanyahu era, Israel has demanded Palestinian recognition of Israel specifically as **"the nation-state of the Jewish people,"** not merely recognition of Israel's existence. Palestinian leadership has resisted this framing, arguing it prejudices the refugee/return question in advance and marginalizes Palestinian citizens of Israel.

**Relevance to confederation**: This is the international/diplomatic mirror of the domestic legal question covered in [[nation-state-law-tension]] (Israel's 2018 Basic Law, Section 1.C).

## 6. Security Arrangements

**The question**: Control of borders, airspace, the Jordan Valley (as a security buffer), and whether a Palestinian state would be permitted a standing military or remain demilitarized.

**Relevance to confederation**: See [[confederation-model-critique]] for the detailed disambiguation of "open borders" (Israel-Palestine crossing control vs. the separate, more extreme proposal of shared external-frontier management) and RAND's finding that Palestinians would want a "standing army" as part of any acceptable two-state arrangement — a demand the same source assesses as very difficult for Israel to accept.

## 7. Gaza — Governance, Demilitarization, Reconstruction

**⚠️ Previously entirely absent from this wiki** — now added per user request.

**The question**: Who governs Gaza after Hamas, under what security guarantees, and how is reconstruction (World Bank estimate: **$80 billion**, up from a prior $53 billion estimate) financed and managed?

**Current status (as of early-mid 2026)**, per [House of Commons Library briefing](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10492/) and [Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/who-will-govern-gaza):

- A US-brokered "20-point peace plan" (per the Trump administration) establishes a **three-tiered structure**:
  1. **Board of Peace** — a US-led international transitional body, chaired by Trump, with an Executive Board of 8 members overseeing a larger international Gaza Executive Board
  2. **National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG)** — a technocratic committee of Palestinian professionals (target: 15 members, 12 confirmed at time of the CFR article) handling day-to-day governance; led by former Palestinian minister Ali Shaath; based in Egypt; **explicitly excludes both Hamas and Palestinian Authority members**
  3. **Palestinian Authority** — expected to eventually assume control after a transitional period extending to **end of 2027**
- Hamas has reportedly indicated willingness to cede governance to the transitional committee
- **Major gaps/challenges**: Israel, Hamas, and the Palestinian Authority itself **did not attend** the January 2026 charter-signing in Davos. Netanyahu raised objections to Turkish participation. Only ~27 of 60 invited nations had joined as of mid-February 2026. Funding mechanisms and the Board's mandate scope remain contested.
- A **multinational security force** is intended to provide enforcement/stabilization during the transition
- The PA itself is separately lobbying for a "major role" in post-war Gaza, "despite the US blueprint" sidelining it initially, per [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/mena/palestinian-authority-plans-for-major-role-in-post-war-gaza) — banking on Arab-state support against Israeli objections

**Relevance to confederation**: **Not addressed at all** in [[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview]] or [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] — a significant gap, since any confederation model presupposes a single, legitimate Palestinian governing partner, which Gaza's current international-transitional-administration status complicates considerably. **This is flagged as a priority gap for future research.**

## 8. Palestinian Political Division (Fatah-Hamas)

**⚠️ Previously entirely absent from this wiki** — now added per user request.

**The question**: Who legitimately represents the Palestinian people in any negotiation or confederative arrangement?

**Background**, per [Washington Institute analysis](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/palestinian-politics-are-more-divided-ever) and [Arab Center DC](https://arabcenterdc.org/resource/the-persistent-illusion-of-palestinian-reconciliation/):

- Split originates in Hamas's 2007 takeover of Gaza, following its 2006 electoral victory — the Palestinian Authority (Fatah-led) has controlled the West Bank only since then, without holding a general election since **2006**
- Over a dozen reconciliation attempts (brokered by Algeria, Egypt, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Turkey, Russia, among others) have **all failed** — root causes described as "ideological, political and security-related" and "too deeply entrenched at this time to be addressed"
- **Current dynamics (2025)**: Fatah/PA faces internal crisis with competing factions challenging President Abbas's leadership; the PA is viewed by **51% of Palestinians as a liability** per cited polling. Hamas seeks to capitalize on Fatah's weakness and (per the source) "public sympathy from the recent conflict," but faces international isolation and significant military degradation post-war.
- **A growing segment of Palestinians reportedly feels alienated from both factions**, rejecting what the source characterizes as their respective "cynical authoritarianism" and ineffectual governance
- Per PCPSR polling cited in the same research, the "overwhelming public priority" among Palestinians is **reunification and free, inclusive elections** — a demand neither faction has delivered

**Relevance to confederation**: This is a **structural precondition problem** for any confederation model, arguably more fundamental than the model's own mechanics. [[confederation-model-critique]] already flags (via Konrad) that Israel's democracy would need to coexist institutionally with what he calls the PA's "single-party dictatorship" — but this actually undersells the problem, since the PA doesn't even have uncontested authority over Gaza, let alone democratic legitimacy via recent elections. **A Land for All's platform and the interview both implicitly assume a single, legitimate Palestinian negotiating partner that does not currently exist in practice.**

## 9. Status of Palestinian Citizens of Israel

**The question**: Civil equality, resource/planning discrimination, and recognition of unrecognized communities (e.g., Bedouin villages in the Negev) — a domestic Israeli civil-rights issue distinct from, but related to, the territorial conflict.

**Relevance to confederation**: [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] documents a specific commitment ("national minority rights, civil equality, appropriate representation") — verified as an accurate quote from the organization's own 2014 platform, matching Chacar's citation in [[confederation-model-critique]].

## 10. Water Resources

**The question**: Israel, the West Bank, and Gaza share the **Mountain Aquifer** (West Bank/Israel) and **Coastal Aquifer** (Gaza/Israel), plus the wider Jordan River basin (shared further with Jordan, Syria, Lebanon).

**Key facts** (per [Fanack Water](https://water.fanack.com/palestine/shared-water-resources-in-palestine/) and other cited sources — **needs direct primary verification**, currently sourced only via search-result synthesis):
- **80-90% of the Mountain Aquifer's recharge area lies in Palestinian territory**, yet historically roughly **80% of its water has been allocated to Israeli use, ~20% to Palestinian use** — a stark inequity given the recharge-area figures
- Regulatory history: the unratified 1955 Johnston Plan, then water provisions within the 1995 Oslo II Agreement
- The region faces a broader accumulating water deficit affecting freshwater availability for both populations

**Relevance to confederation**: [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] mentions "shared aquifers" only in passing (as an example of "interdependence" in the current 2s1h.org site's general framing) — **not developed with any specific allocation mechanism**, unlike the more precise citizenship/Jerusalem provisions in the 2014 founding document. This is a genuine gap in the confederation platform's specificity.
