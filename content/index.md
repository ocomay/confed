## Framework & Methodology

- [[confederation-spectrum-framework]] — **Read this first.** The project's guiding methodology: confederation is treated as a multidimensional spectrum, not a single fixed proposal. States 6 foundational assumptions (including a new one on gradual, trust-building transition — see below), carries a compact dashboard, and links to 4 thematic sub-pages with full detail. Key findings: ALFA is a genuinely mixed, intermediate position (not simply "moderate"); classic two-state proposals are more separative than ALFA on several axes despite both being called "two-state"; Area C Palestinians and Gaza residents already have no functioning representation today — annexation's core function is to make this permanent.
- [[axes-governance-representation]] — Detailed comparison (5 configurations): parliamentary structure, Palestinian political unity, Gaza governance, self-determination symmetry.
- [[axes-territory-borders-security]] — Detailed comparison: border permeability, external-frontier control, Jerusalem governance, settler/settlement status, security integration.
- [[axes-citizenship-rights]] — Detailed comparison: citizenship/immigration symmetry, refugee return scope, Palestinian citizens of Israel's equality.
- [[axes-resources-economy]] — Detailed comparison: water resource allocation, economic integration.

## Concepts

- [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]] — The proposed "third way" political structure: two sovereign states + shared institutional layer + freedom of movement. Now includes reconciliation against A Land for All's actual published platform (several discrepancies found — refugee provisions, settler mechanics, Jerusalem governance).
- [[comparative-conflict-resolutions]] — Bosnia, Northern Ireland, South Africa and other precedents cited as inspiration for the confederation model (flagged as needing independent verification).
- [[confederation-model-critique]] — Full-text-sourced critique from two directions: Palestinian-rights/progressive (Chacar, +972, 2019 — model doesn't go far enough) and realist/security (Konrad, +972, 2021; RAND — model asks too much). Both converge on flagging the ~30x economic/power asymmetry between populations. Corrects a mischaracterization of what the RAND study actually measured.
- [[israeli-palestinian-public-opinion-polling]] — **⚠️ Contains a major correction**: earlier cited INSS percentages (confederation support, two-state support, etc.) were found unverifiable against the actual ingested source and have been retracted. Only RAND's confirmed findings (deep mutual distrust, ~60% Israeli status-quo preference) remain load-bearing.
- [[israeli-right-wing-opposition]] — The dominant Israeli right-wing position, which rejects Palestinian sovereignty in any form (confederation included), favoring West Bank annexation instead. Polling cross-reference retracted pending better sourcing.
- [[nation-state-law-tension]] — How the confederation model reconciles (or doesn't) with Israel's 2018 Basic Law defining national self-determination as unique to the Jewish people, and the Law of Return. Includes primary Basic Law text and the precise "as they see fit" citizenship-discretion mechanism from A Land for All's own platform.
- [[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]] — Direct quotes from A Land for All's own 2014 founding principles and current website, compared against the interview and Wikipedia. **Resolves a real discrepancy**: Jerusalem would be jointly managed by Israel and Palestine (per primary source), not by "international authorities and religious representatives" as Wikipedia claimed.
- [[final-status-core-disputes]] — Framework page: the ten core disputes underlying the whole conflict (borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, recognition, security, Gaza, Fatah-Hamas split, Palestinian citizens of Israel, water), used as background context for evaluating any proposed solution. Fills two previously-missing gaps: Gaza governance (current Board of Peace / NCAG transitional structure) and the Fatah-Hamas political division.

## Open questions across the wiki

- **Polling data gap**: [[israeli-palestinian-public-opinion-polling]] currently has NO verified confederation-specific support percentages for Israelis or Palestinians. This is a real, acknowledged gap — needs direct ingestion of PCPSR primary reports, Gallup's full page, or INSS's actual Infogram chart data (not accessible via simple page fetch).
- **Jerusalem governance discrepancy — RESOLVED**: Primary source ([[a-land-for-all-primary-platform]]) confirms joint Israeli-Palestinian municipal management, correcting Wikipedia's "international authorities and religious representatives" claim.
- **Refugee provision discrepancy — clarified**: Primary source confirms citizenship remains "as they see fit" for each state; refugees get a gradual *residency* option in Israel, not citizenship. See [[nation-state-law-tension]].
- **Gaza governance — now covered**: See [[final-status-core-disputes]] for the current (2026) Board of Peace / National Committee for the Administration of Gaza transitional structure. Still not integrated into the confederation-model pages specifically — A Land for All's platform doesn't address how Gaza's current international-transitional status interacts with the confederation proposal.
- **Fatah-Hamas division — now covered**: See [[final-status-core-disputes]]. Raises a structural question not addressed anywhere else in the wiki: does a legitimate, unified Palestinian negotiating partner currently exist to implement any confederation agreement with?
- **Water resources — only briefly covered**: [[final-status-core-disputes]] notes the Mountain Aquifer's 80/20 Israeli/Palestinian allocation split despite 80-90% of recharge occurring in Palestinian territory; deserves a dedicated page.
- **Borders/land-swap mechanics — thin**: Only the Green Line baseline is established; no source yet addresses specific land-swap proposals.
- **Comparative precedents underdeveloped**: [[comparative-conflict-resolutions]] cases (Bosnia, N. Ireland, S. Africa, Lebanon) are mentioned only in passing in the source and need dedicated research to assess their actual relevance as models.
- **Right-wing opposition page thin**: [[israeli-right-wing-opposition]] relies on secondary news characterization rather than primary party platforms/statements — needs expansion.
- **Hamas/Palestinian Islamist perspective missing entirely**: No source yet represents this viewpoint on confederation or alternative solutions — now more pressing given [[final-status-core-disputes]]'s Gaza/Fatah-Hamas content.
- **No primary Israeli security-establishment perspective yet**: The realist critique in [[confederation-model-critique]] is journalistic, not from security officials directly.
- **Framework axis-sufficiency unresolved**: Not yet established whether ~12 axes are sufficient, redundant, or missing major dimensions (e.g., religious/holy-site access as distinct from general Jerusalem governance).
- **Two new reference configurations introduced without dedicated pages**: "State of all its citizens" (Bishara/Balad) and classic "two states for two peoples" (Clinton Parameters-style) are now compared in [[confederation-spectrum-framework]]'s table but have no standalone profile pages yet — the table's characterizations are sourced from general knowledge/brief search summaries, not deep primary research. Candidates for dedicated pages if this comparison proves useful going forward.
- **Gaza and Fatah-Hamas remain undertheorized across all alternative models**: [[confederation-spectrum-framework]]'s comparison table found that none of full annexation, the civic-state model, or A Land for All has a well-sourced answer for Gaza governance or Palestinian political unity — only the status quo column is well-specified on these two axes.
