## Confirmed Findings (from full-text ingested sources)

### RAND Corporation study (2018–2019 fieldwork, published Feb 2021)
See [[confederation-model-critique]] for full detail. Confirmed, source-verified findings:
- **~60% of Israelis** said the status quo could feasibly continue as it has for decades and was preferable to risking any alternative
- Palestinians would accept a **modified two-state solution** (contiguity, autonomy, standing army, border control) — but researchers assess this "would be very difficult for Israel to accept"
- Methodology: 273 individuals, four-hour focus-group discussions, Delphi method, fieldwork **2018–2019** (i.e., pre-dates October 7, 2023 by several years — findings should not be assumed to still hold post-war without updating)
- The study covered **five alternatives**: two-state, one-state, confederation, Israeli annexation of Area C, status quo — **no confederation-specific percentage figures appear in the press release itself**
- **Deep mutual distrust** confirmed as a top-line finding: "the data highlight the deep distrust and profound animosity of each side for the other" (Daniel Egel, lead author)
- Educational intervention effect: pre-testing showed few participants started with clear understanding of the alternatives; a brief overview shifted some opinions — suggesting **public opinion on these options is not fixed and may be sensitive to how alternatives are explained**, a point relevant to evaluating [[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview]]'s advocacy approach

## Implications for the Confederation Model (revised, conservative version)

Reading the **confirmed** data against [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]] and [[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview]]:

1. **The interview presents no polling data of its own** — its optimism about trust-building is not tested against any external figures within the interview itself.
2. **What RAND actually establishes**: deep mutual distrust, low confidence in any alternative to the status quo (not confederation specifically), and — notably — that Palestinians would need a two-state model with more robust sovereignty guarantees (standing army, border control) than Israel is likely to accept. This is a real empirical tension the interview does not address, but it is a **two-state-general** finding, not confederation-specific.
3. **We do not currently have verified data on confederation-specific support levels** — this is a real gap in the wiki, not a settled fact one way or the other. Prior claims that confederation is a declining/minority position should not be repeated without better sourcing.
4. RAND's finding that **brief educational explanation shifted some opinions** in pre-testing is relevant to assessing the interview's advocacy strategy — it suggests unfamiliarity with confederation specifically (as opposed to settled opposition) may be a real factor, which cuts somewhat *against* the "public won't accept it" framing in [[confederation-model-critique]]'s discussion of Konrad's "low awareness" point — worth holding both in mind.
