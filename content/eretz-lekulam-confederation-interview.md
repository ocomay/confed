# Interview: "A New Land — Is There a Chance for Confederation?" (DemocratTV, Jan 2025)

**Summary**: Source summary of a January 2025 interview on DemocratTV between host Ruth Klein and Prof. Oren Yiftachel and Dr. Rula Hardal of the organization "A Land for All" (ארץ לכולם), presenting their confederation-based vision for resolving the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

## Source Context

**Format**: Studio interview, ~20 minutes  
**Host**: Ruth Klein  
**Guests**:
- Prof. Oren Yiftachel — political/legal geographer, Ben-Gurion University; co-founder of [[a-land-for-all]]

**Publication date**: January 2, 2025 — i.e., roughly 15 months after the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack and during the subsequent Gaza war. The interview explicitly frames itself as asking whether hope for a shared future is possible "specifically after October 7."

**⚠️ Source nature — advocacy interview, not neutral analysis**: This is a promotional/advocacy interview conducted by guests representing a single organization's platform. It should be read as **one proposed solution being advocated for**, not as a balanced survey of positions. Claims made by the guests (especially about "where the problem lies") reflect their organization's perspective and are **not independently verified** in this source. See flagged claims below.

## Core Content Summary

The interview lays out the [[israel-palestine-confederation-model|confederation model]] promoted by [[a-land-for-all]]: neither full separation (two closed states, walls) nor full unification (one state), but two independent political entities with a shared layer of cooperation (environment, economy, security) and freedom of movement.

Key claims and arguments are broken out into their own concept pages:

- [[a-land-for-all]] — the organization, its history and platform

## Contested / Flagged Claims

Per wiki citation rules, claims presented as fact by the speakers but which represent a contested or one-sided framing are flagged here rather than stated as settled fact on concept pages:

1. **"The problem is primarily on the Israeli side"** (Rula Hardal, ~4:35–9:00): Hardal argues that in the last 15 years, Israeli governments (she dates this from Netanyahu's 2009 government) have moved away from a political settlement and denied Palestinian self-determination, while the PLO/Palestinian Authority has not changed its official platform (accepting a Palestinian state on ~22% of historical Palestine, i.e., the West Bank and Gaza) since the late 1980s/Oslo era. **This is a contested framing** — it is the speaker's characterization and does not account for, e.g., the collapse of the Camp David 2000 and Taba 2001 talks, the Second Intifada, disputes over the 2008 Olmert offer, Hamas's 2007 takeover of Gaza, or differing Israeli accounts of why negotiations stalled. Needs verification / counter-perspective from other sources.

2. **"The Palestinian Authority does nothing but coordinate with Israel, so the 'no partner' claim is false"** (Hardal, ~8:38–9:24): Presented as a rebuttal to the Israeli "no partner for peace" narrative. This is an advocacy claim; the "no partner" debate is a long-standing, deeply contested one in Israeli and Palestinian politics with substantial argumentation on multiple sides. Needs verification / additional sourcing.

3. **Characterization of settlers as "colonial occupiers who kill and trample"** (Yiftachel, ~11:14): A strongly value-laden characterization presented without qualification. Reflects the speaker's political position; other sources would characterize the settler population very differently. Flagged as opinion, not fact.

4. **ICJ ruling on illegality of Israeli presence in the territories** (Yiftachel, ~18:17–18:40): Yiftachel references an ICJ advisory opinion from "this summer" (2024) declaring Israel's presence in the territories illegal. **This appears to reference the real July 2024 ICJ advisory opinion** on the legal consequences of Israel's policies in the Occupied Palestinian Territory — but the characterization of its content and implications here is the speaker's own summary, not a direct citation. Needs verification against the actual ICJ text.

5. **Framing of Hamas** (Yiftachel, ~9:51–10:07): Yiftachel calls Hamas "a movement of murderous and extreme Islam" but then pivots to argue "the Palestinian side is not innocent either, to put it very gently" — a notably soft framing of Hamas's role in October 7 relative to the strength of the initial characterization. The interview does not explore Hamas's covenant, October 7 planning, or hostage-taking in any depth. Flagged as underdeveloped in this source.

6. **Claim that pre-Intifada (1970s–mid-1990s) period had "normal life" and freedom of movement without checkpoints** (Hardal, ~16:39–17:22): Presented as a nostalgic reference point for what confederation-like conditions might resemble. This is a broad historical generalization about a multi-decade period that included the 1978-82 settlement expansion, the 1987 First Intifada's outbreak, and significant friction; needs more precise historical sourcing.
