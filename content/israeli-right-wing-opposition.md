# Israeli Right-Wing Opposition to Palestinian Sovereignty

**Summary**: The dominant Israeli right-wing/settler-movement political position — opposition to any form of Palestinian sovereign statehood (confederation included), favoring instead annexation of West Bank territory. Provides the political-reality counterweight to [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]], which does not engage with this position at all.

## Core Position

A significant and politically dominant current in Israeli politics — spanning Likud and further-right parties, and closely tied to the settler movement's political body (the Yesha Council) — holds that **no Palestinian state should be established** in the West Bank ("Judea and Samaria" in this political vocabulary) under any model, confederative or otherwise.

This is a **categorically different objection** from the realist/security critique in [[confederation-model-critique]] (which grants that Palestinian statehood might be desirable but flags implementation obstacles). The right-wing position rejects the premise itself — Palestinian sovereignty over any part of the land west of the Jordan River is treated as unacceptable regardless of proposed security arrangements, institutional design, or confederative safeguards.

## Stated Rationale (as found in sources)

- The vision, associated historically with Likud, treats Israeli sovereignty as properly extending "from the Mediterranean to the Jordan River" — i.e., the full territory, without a co-sovereign Palestinian state anywhere within it.
- Naftali Bennett (former Yesha Council head, former PM) is cited as a representative figure who has "repeated on numerous occasions his staunch, unequivocal opposition to the creation of a Palestinian state."

## Alternative Proposed by This Camp

Rather than confederation or a negotiated two-state solution, this position's proposed alternative is **annexation of Area C** — the roughly 60% of the West Bank under full Israeli civil and security control per the Oslo Accords' territorial division, where the large majority of Israeli settlers live. Annexation of Area C would, in this framing, foreclose the territorial contiguity a Palestinian state would need, effectively ending the two-state framework (confederative or classic) as a live option.

## Why This Matters for Evaluating the Confederation Model

[[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview]] does not address this political current at all — the interview discusses trust-building, institutional design, and international law, but does not engage with the question of whether a plurality-or-more of the Israeli political system and public actively rejects the premise of Palestinian statehood in any form. Any assessment of the confederation model's feasibility needs to account for:

1. This is not merely public *skepticism* to be won over with better arguments (the [[eretz-lekulam-confederation-interview|interview's]] implicit framing) — it is an **organized political position** with representation in government coalitions and the settler movement's institutional infrastructure.
2. The settler population living within any future Palestinian confederative state (addressed in [[confederation-model-critique]] as a governance risk) is not merely a logistical detail — it is the demographic and political base of the movement most opposed to the entire premise of the plan.
