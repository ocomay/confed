This page goes to the organization's **own platform documents directly** — both the original 2014 founding text and the current (2026) website — to get as close to primary sourcing as currently possible, and to check whether the platform itself has **changed over time** (2014 → today) independent of how any single spokesperson or secondary source describes it.

## Direct Quotes: 2014 "Shared and Agreed Founding Principles"

### Self-determination
> "Two sovereign states will exist – Palestine and Israel, where the two people will realize their right of self-determination."

**Note**: This 2014 language does **not** explicitly use "Jewish state" terminology — it frames self-determination symmetrically ("the two people," plural, each realizing self-determination) without specifying Israel's character as uniquely Jewish or Palestine's as uniquely Arab/Palestinian. This is a notably more neutral formulation than Israel's own Basic Law (see [[nation-state-law-tension]]), which explicitly claims self-determination "unique to the Jewish people" specifically **within Israel**. The platform doesn't contradict Israel's Jewish character, but it also doesn't affirm it in the same exclusive terms — it simply doesn't engage with that specific legal framing at all.

### Immigration and citizenship — the key mechanism
> "Both countries will have the right to determine the nature of immigration into them, and their citizenship laws. Therefore, Palestine will be free to grant citizenship to Palestinian refugees, and the State of Israel will be free to grant citizenship to diaspora Jews, **as they see fit**."

**This is the precise mechanism reconciling confederation with the Law of Return** (see [[nation-state-law-tension]]): each state retains **full, undiluted control** over its own immigration and citizenship law. Israel's Law of Return is not merely "preserved" as this wiki's earlier pages characterized it — it is explicitly reaffirmed as within Israel's **sovereign discretion**, symmetrically mirrored by the Palestinian state's discretion to grant citizenship to Palestinian refugees. Neither state is obligated by the confederation structure to grant citizenship or return rights to the other's population.

**Important nuance this resolves**: This confirms and sharpens what [[a-land-for-all]] (via Wikipedia) previously described as a "gradual option to reside in Israel" for Palestinian refugees — that refers to **residency**, not **citizenship**. The 2014 principles are explicit that *citizenship* law remains each state's own prerogative "as they see fit" — meaning Israel is not committed by this platform to grant citizenship to any specific number of Palestinian refugees, only (per other platform language) to permit residency under negotiated/gradual terms.

### Jerusalem — a THIRD, more specific formulation
> "Jerusalem will serve as the capital of both states... A special municipal government will be established in it, **managed jointly and equally by both states**."

**⚠️ This directly conflicts with Wikipedia's characterization** (used in [[a-land-for-all]] and [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]]), which described Jerusalem as "managed by international authorities and religious representatives." The **primary source says joint Israeli-Palestinian municipal management** — not international or religious third-party administration. Three distinct formulations now on record:

| Source | Jerusalem framing |
|---|---|
| Jan 2025 interview (Yiftachel) | "Brussels model" — shared by multiple national communities, general comparison |
| Wikipedia (secondary synthesis) | Managed by **international authorities and religious representatives** |
| 2014 primary platform | Joint municipal government, **managed jointly and equally by both states** — i.e., Israeli-Palestinian, not international/religious |

**This is a genuine discrepancy needing resolution.** The primary 2014 text supports Yiftachel's general framing (shared administration between the two national communities) far better than it supports Wikipedia's "international/religious representatives" claim. **Correction**: [[a-land-for-all]] and [[israel-palestine-confederation-model]] should be updated to prefer the primary source's "joint, equal Israeli-Palestinian municipal government" framing over Wikipedia's international/religious characterization, pending further verification. It's possible Wikipedia's editors drew on a different/later iteration of the platform, or conflated A Land for All's proposal with a different peace plan's Jerusalem provisions — **needs direct verification**.

### Settlers and refugees — residency, not citizenship, in the other state
> "Israelis, including those who currently live in the territories where Palestine will be established, [would be permitted] to live as permanent residents of Palestine" — and Palestinians similarly permitted to live as permanent residents in Israel — "provided they accept the host state's sovereignty and laws."

Confirms the residency-not-citizenship framing already documented in [[a-land-for-all]] (settlers become "Israeli residents of Palestine," not Palestinian citizens) — and clarifies this is explicitly **symmetrical**: Palestinians could equally become "Palestinian residents of Israel" under the same logic, subject to Israeli law.

### Palestinian citizens of Israel — matches Chacar's direct quote
> "Palestinian-Arab citizens of Israel will be granted national minority rights, civil equality, appropriate representation in government institutions."

This **exactly matches** the phrase Chacar quoted in her 2019 critique (see [[confederation-model-critique]]) — confirming her citation was accurate to the primary source, not a paraphrase.

## Direct Quotes: Current Website (2s1h.org, accessed 2026)

The organization's site has moved (alandforall.org now redirects to 2s1h.org) and the framing has shifted noticeably toward **more general, principle-level language** and away from the 2014 document's specific mechanisms:

### Self-determination — more universalist framing than 2014
> "Any lasting resolution must affirm the **equal right of both nations** to self-determination."
> "National self-determination without domination, shared governance without erasing identity, and security without subjugation."

**This is notably vaguer than the 2014 text** — no specific mention of immigration/citizenship mechanisms, no Jerusalem specifics, no explicit settler/refugee provisions in the fetched excerpt. It reads as a mission-statement-level summary rather than a detailed platform.

### Jerusalem, settlements, refugees — explicitly deferred, not specified
> The organization "rejects zero-sum thinking on complex issues like Jerusalem, settlements, and refugees, and instead promotes creative and fair solutions" — **without providing specific positions on these topics** in the fetched content.

**This is a significant shift from 2014**: the founding principles gave concrete mechanisms (joint Jerusalem government, residency-not-citizenship for settlers/refugees, "as they see fit" immigration discretion). The current site appears to **step back from these specifics**, framing them instead as areas for "creative and fair solutions" without committing to the 2014 document's precise formulations. **This could mean**: (a) the org has deliberately generalized its public messaging while retaining detailed positions elsewhere (e.g., in a fuller policy document not surfaced in this fetch), (b) the org's actual positions have evolved/softened on specifics since 2014, or (c) this particular page is just a summary landing page and detailed positions exist elsewhere on the site. **Needs further navigation of 2s1h.org to resolve** — this page's fetch may not have captured the full site.

### Freedom of movement — softer than 2014's "as they see fit" citizenship language
> "Gradual steps toward freedom of movement and residence for all Israelis and Palestinians across our shared homeland."

Consistent with 2014's residency framework, expressed more generally.

## Assessment: How the Interview, Wikipedia, and Primary Sources Relate

| Element | 2014 Platform (primary) | Current Site (primary, 2026) | Wikipedia (secondary) | Jan 2025 Interview |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-determination | "Two people... realize right of self-determination" | "Equal right of both nations to self-determination" | Not directly quoted | Not framed in these exact legal terms |
| Immigration/citizenship | Each state decides "as they see fit" | Not specified in fetched excerpt | Diaspora Jews "maintain right to return" (Aliyah); Palestinian state has "sovereign right to grant... return" | "Return to Palestinian state," "freedom of movement" for visits |
| Jerusalem | Joint municipal gov't, equal Israeli-Palestinian management | Deferred ("creative solutions," unspecified) | International authorities + religious representatives | "Brussels" — shared by multiple communities |
| Settlers | Permanent Palestine residents, under Palestinian law | Not specified in fetched excerpt | Israeli residents of Palestine, vote locally only | "Under Palestinian law," contrasted with "colonial occupiers" |

**Overall conclusion**: The **2014 founding document is the most detailed and specific source available**, and should be treated as the most authoritative baseline for the platform's actual mechanics going forward in this wiki. Wikipedia's Jerusalem characterization appears to be an **error or reflects a different source** not matching the 2014 primary text. The current 2026 website has moved toward **vaguer, more aspirational language**, possibly reflecting either message strategy evolution or incomplete content capture in this fetch.
